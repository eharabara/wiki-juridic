Inﬂation development
In the third quarter of 2017, the average annual inﬂation rate
was 7.4 percent, being by 0.3 percentage points higher than in
the previous quarter.
Compared to the signiﬁcant increase in
the ﬁrst half of this year, during the third quarter of 2017, the
annual inﬂation rate dynamics was less pronounced.
Thus, it
maintained at the level of 7.3 percent in July and August, then, it
increased to 7.6 percent in September. As a result, during the third
quarter of 2017, the annual inﬂation rate continued to be above the
inﬂation target set in the medium-term Monetary Policy Strategy.
Maintenance of an upward trajectory of annual CPI inﬂation rate
was mainly driven by food products’ prices accumulated pressures
as a result of less favorable agriculture-meteorological conditions
for local fruit and vegetables this year. At the same time, the
high inﬂation ﬁgures were also sustained by the impact of tariffs’
increases on some regulated services, especially at the beginning
of this year. The basic inﬂation experienced a relatively stable
dynamic, maintaining close to the inﬂation target. The trend of
appreciation of the national currency, during the reference period,
has mitigated the inﬂuence of pro-inﬂationary factors mentioned
above. According to estimates, the domestic demand has had a
negligible impact on inﬂation over the reference period. Deviation
between the effective annual inﬂation rate in the third quarter
of 2017 and the anticipated value within the forecasting round
pertaining to the Inﬂation Report no. 3, 2017 was a null one.

Economic developments
The annual dynamics of economic activity, in the second quarter of
2017, was slightly lower than at the beginning of this year, mainly,
as a result of the dissipation of positive effects generated by the
previous year’s harvest, which fueled the increase in exports in
the previous periods. Thus, in the second quarter of 2017, GDP
grew by 2.5 percent compared to the same period of 2016. The
domestic demand contribution became more pronounced than
in the previous years. Thus, the annual rate of gross formation
of ﬁxed capital accelerated up to 6.3 percent, by 3.0 percentage
points above the level of the beginning of the current year. Similar
to the previous periods, the contribution of the component to the
change in stocks at GDP dynamics continued to be a pronounced
positive one, attaining the level of 2.7 percentage points.
In
the second quarter of 2017, ﬁnal consumption of households
experienced an annual dynamic of 3.8 percent. Final consumption
of public administration recorded a negligible evolution during
the reference period, decreasing by 0.1 percent compared to the
second quarter of 2016. At the same time, the positive impact
of external demand declined considerably compared to previous
periods.
In this way, exports of goods and services increased
by only 7.3 percent compared to the second quarter of 2016.
Though to a lesser extent than at the beginning of the year, the
economic activity was substantially tempered by the evolution
of imports. Thus, they were by 11.1 percent higher than those
in the second quarter of 2016. By categories of resources, GDP
growth was largely determined by the positive contribution of
1.6 percentage points generated by the increase of net taxes on
the product. Positive developments with noticeable impact on
GDP dynamics were recorded in trade, transport and storage and
construction. The industrial sector experienced a stagnation in
the second quarter of 2017, while the added value related to
agriculture decreased by 6.9 percent.
Monetary policy
During the third quarter of 2017, three meetings of the Executive
Board of the National Bank of Moldova on monetary policy
decisions were held. As a result of the assessment of the balance
of internal and external risks and of the short and medium term
inﬂation expectations, the Executive Board of the National Bank of
Moldova decided at its meeting from 26 July 2017 to maintain the
base rate applied to the main monetary policy operations at the
level of 8.0 percent annually. Subsequently, at the meetings of 28
August 2017, it was decided to reduce the base rate applied to
the main monetary policy operations by 0.5 percentage points,
from 8.0 percent (level set at the meeting on 28 June 2017) to 7.5
percent per year.
At its meeting on September 27, 2017, the
Executive Board of the National Bank of Moldova decided to
maintain the base rate applied to the main monetary policy
operations at 7.5 percent per year.

Medium-term inﬂation forecasting
According to the current forecasting round, the GDP deviation
will register a sluggish recovery for the entire forecasting period.
However, it will remain in the negative zone, near the "0" value, for
the entire forecasting period. Therefore, placing economic activity
below its equilibrium level will cause disinﬂationary pressures on
behalf of aggregate demand.
The real monetary conditions will have a restrictive impact on
demand. The actual effective exchange rate will feed a restrictive
nature of monetary conditions throughout the whole forecast
horizon.
At the same time, the real interest rate will be
stimulating for the entire forecast horizon, with the exception of
three consecutive quarters, starting in the fourth quarter of 2018,
when it will feed a restrictive nature of monetary conditions.
The annual inﬂation rate, starting with the fourth quarter of 2017,
will decline rapidly and will return, beginning with the ﬁrst quarter
of 2018, within the interval for two consecutive quarters. As of the
third quarter of 2018, for three consecutive quarters, the annual
inﬂation rate will place below the lower limit of the range, after
which, as of the second quarter of 2019, it will return within the
interval and will place in the proximity of inﬂation target at the end
of the forecast horizon. The maximum value of 7.3 percent will be
recorded in the fourth quarter of 2017, and the minimum value
of 2.6 percent – in the fourth quarter of 2018. Compared to the
values recorded in the Inﬂation Report no. 3, 2017, the projected
annual average annual inﬂation rates are similar for the current
year and are below by 0.4 percentage points for the year 2018.

Estimation of the economic activity’s development by means of the Dynamic Factor Model 
The NBM’s analysis and forecasting system is a framework that facilitates the estimation of the monetary 
policy’s impact on economic activity and inflation, as well as the elaboration of proposals on the measures to be 
taken to achieve the set inflation target. Similar to other central banks to implement the inflation targeting 
regime, the NBM’s analysis and forecasting system, which underlies the decision-making process, includes some 
indispensable elements such as maintaining a common database, estimating economic activity for the current 
period, preparing the short-term inflation forecast, the development of a medium-term forecasting model, the 
assessment of risks and uncertainties in different sectors, reporting and communication procedures etc. 
The assessment of economic activity for the current period allows to identify inflationary pressures generated 
by excess demand, and acts as a starting point for the medium-term inflation forecasting. However, this 
exercise is hampered by the fact that some indicators, in this particular case – the GDP, are presented by the 
statistical authority with a certain delay. Thus, GDP is published 75 days after the end of the reference period. At 
the same time, many high-frequency indicators are published in a prompt manner and provide decision-makers 
with relevant information on the state of the economy. The large number of indicators, regular revisions and 
measurement errors, as well as their publication at different time periods, the above factors all combined create 
confusion and difficulty for the correct interpreting of available information.    
There is a variety of approaches to calculate the economic development indicator by means of high-frequency 
indicators. One of these methods is the Dynamic Factor Model. Being based on the Kalman filter algorithm, the 
method estimates, within the available sample, a common unobservable variable that determines the evolution 
of other observable variables, such as GDP and some high-frequency up-to-date indicators. Next, on the basis of 
up-to-date indicators, the value of this unobservable variable is estimated for the current period, after which, 
based on coefficients estimated within the available sample, the GDP value for the current period is determined.

• Uncertain risk on oil price developments. The forecast
on oil price developments has low volatility trajectory, but
uncertainties of oil market are not to be neglected, especially
if they suggest an opposite development of the situation.
The implementation and possible extension of the OPEC+
agreement on the reduction of oil production raises many
questions, given that the degree of implication of the
participants in the agreement differs. At the same time, the
U.S. is acting as an independent player on the oil market,
and, against the background of rising oil prices, the shale

thus contributing to global oil
production growth and the rise in international oil prices. At
the same time, the successful implementation of the OPEC+
agreement and a potential escalation of the geopolitical
conﬂict in the Kurdish region could generate the rise in
international oil prices (uncertainty).
• Increased
number
of
events
taking
place
in
the
international forex market.
In the current forecasting
round, the USD/EUR parity has a relatively steady projection,
but, at the moment, many events are taking place, which in
the coming months will contribute to the volatility of the USD
and the European single currency.
At the October 2017
meeting, the European Central Bank will decide the fate of
the Quantitative Easing Program. Most experts anticipate the
halving of monthly asset purchases, provided the annual
inﬂation is steadily rising but has not yet reached the ECB
target. Also, the political events in the European Union will
inﬂuence the volatility of the European single currency over
the next quarters. At the same time, the volatility of the USD
depends on the appointment of the new President of the
Federal Reserve System, on the implementation of the Fiscal
Burden
Reduction
Project,
and
on
possible
trade
protectionism acts conducted by the U.S. through their
foreign policy. At the same time, the international market
looks forward to the outcome of the Fed meeting in
December 2017, on the assumption that the expected
increase in the Fed’s interest rate band has been already
included in the current USD value. Also, the international
market anticipates that the Fed will increase the interest rate
band three more times in 2018 (uncertainty).
Real sector
• Weather-sensitivity of domestic prices on local fruit
and
vegetables
following
the
adverse
weather
incident. Based on the recent years’ experience, prices for
domestic fruit and vegetables are greatly affected by
adverse
weather
conditions
such
as
frosts,
droughts,
abundant rainfalls, by costs associated with harvesting,
transportation, storage, but also by their marketing on local
markets. An early frost would cause rising of food prices
above the projected values over the next few months
(pro-inﬂationary).
• Uncertainties regarding the 2018 harvest. According
to the latest forecasts of the State Hydrometeorological
Service (SHS) and the preliminary information gathered, the
2017 harvest’s volumes of wheat, sunﬂower, and maize will
be above the average recorded in recent years, but lower
than in the last year. The next year’s harvest and, hence, the
domestic food prices will depend, to a large extent, on
agrometeorological conditions of the coming months and the
next year. However, as it is difﬁcult to ﬁrmly project any
weather developments, this fact will lend a high level of
uncertainty to the 2018 food prices forecast.
Therefore,
given the high weather-sensitivity of agricultural production,
the dynamics of domestic food prices in 2018 will be
characterized by a high level of uncertainty (uncertainty).

• Uncertainties regarding the timing and the scope of
adjustments to be made to the regulated services
tariffs. The medium-term inﬂation outlook is based on an
assumption of a signiﬁcant cut in utility tariffs for electricity
and gas, in 2018, driven by the fall in energy prices on the
regional market, as well as on signiﬁcant increases in tariffs
for certain services, to be implemented in 2019, as a result
of changes made to forecast assumptions, as well as certain
ﬁnancial deviations in several domestic economy sectors.
Since the level of tariffs on some regulated services has a
considerable social impact, the timing of their adjustment
can be determined based not only on economic factors but
also
on

objectives
pursued
by
decision-makers. Therefore, the timing and scope of tariff
adjustments cannot be projected with a sufﬁcient degree of
conﬁdence (uncertainty).
• Existing
pre-conditions
for
lowering
heating
fees.
Although within the inﬂation forecast horizon there are
pre-conditions for lowering heating fees by about 10 percent
due to positive ﬁnancial deviations recorded, this assumption
was
not
included
in
the
inﬂation
projection
because
international ﬁnancial institutions express controversial
opinions on this topic. However, in case of the ﬁrming up of
these pre-conditions for fee lowering, the inﬂation projection
will be revised downwards (disinﬂationary).
• Potential decrease of medicine prices.
Following the
adoption of additional regulations and as a result of the
domestic currency appreciation over the current year,
medicine prices recorded signiﬁcant decreases. In the event
of any changes in the range of medicines included in the
Medicine Price Catalog, but also if the current trend of the
exchange rate dynamics remains unchanged, the medicine
price
could
decrease
by
about
5.0
to
10.0
percent
(disinﬂationary).
• The domestic economy’s loss of competitive strength
as a result of the appreciation, in real terms, of the
domestic currency. The recent appreciation trend, in real
terms, of the domestic currency exchange rate against the
currencies of the main trading partners, which is likely to
continue in the medium term as a result of a series of
fundamental factors, could have a signiﬁcant impact on
trade and aggregate demand (disinﬂationary).
• Public system reform. Following the implementation of
the public system reform, it was suggested to optimize the
number of employees working in public sector. A potential
reduction
of
public
employees’
number,
if
improperly
implemented, could result in labor market tensions and
cause the decline of aggregate demand (disinﬂationary).
• Delaying investments in infrastructure. The projection
assumes that local governments will make signiﬁcant
investments in infrastructure. If these are postponed, the
level of economic activity and inﬂation will have to be
revised downwards (disinﬂationary).
• Adoption of the Meal Vouchers Law. The use of vouchers
to purchase food and food services will increase, in real
terms, the earnings and the purchasing power of employees,
will redirect part of salary incomes (equivalent to the value


48
Inﬂation Report (BNM, no. 4, November 2017)
of issued vouchers) to the purchasing of other goods and
services (pro-inﬂationary).
• Potential faster recovery of aggregate demand. The
medium-term inﬂation projection is based on the assumption
of a modest aggregate demand that has minor impact on
prices. An eventual increase in economic activity could exert
further inﬂationary pressures on core inﬂation and some food
prices (pro-inﬂationary).
Monetary and public sectors
• Excess
liquidity
in
the
banking
sector.
Following
monetary emissions in 2016 - 2017, as a result of the NBM’s
interventions on foreign exchange market (foreign exchange
purchases) totaling net amount of 773.4 million USD (approx.
14.58 billion MDL), the excess liquidity has accumulated in
the banking system in the amount of approx. 7.3 billion MDL;
in the short term, the excess liquidity may have a positive
impact on aggregate demand, foreign exchange rate, as well
as
on
the
strengthening
of
inﬂationary
pressures
(pro-inﬂationary).
• Pension reform. The Social Fund allocations provided to
low-income
population
will
stimulate
its
consumption
spending, hence sustaining the domestic aggregate demand
and imports, and creating downward pressures on the
Moldovan lei exchange rate (pro-inﬂationary).
• The Treasury Single Account balances. Over 2017, the
Ministry of Finances has accumulated balances in the
Treasury Single Account (TSA). As the Parliamentary elections
are to be held in 2018, there is a high probability that the
Government of the Republic of Moldova will spend the
excessive
ﬁnancial
means
accumulated
in
the
TSA;
otherwise, the accumulated TSA balances will strongly
impact the evolution of ﬁscal indicators, and will generate
additional disinﬂationary pressures (disinﬂationary).
• Weak lending of economy.
The loan market has been
experiencing pessimistic developments during 2017. At the
same time, it should be mentioned that the maximum
decline in lending activity was recorded in September 2016
(-16.4 percent per annum), after which the trend switched to
ascendant (-6.4 percent per annum, in September 2017).
The low corporate lending activity is conditioned by either
the economic and political instability in the Republic of
Moldova and the region, or by unacceptable credit conditions
and associated high risks. The decline in lending activity
could create additional disinﬂationary pressures, but it
stimulates banks to look for proﬁtable alternatives to
generate revenues on the market (investments in securities,
foreign exchange transactions, etc.)
(disinﬂationary with
pro-inﬂationary potential).
