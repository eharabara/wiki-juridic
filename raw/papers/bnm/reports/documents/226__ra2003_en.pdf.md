1. Economic Situation of the Republic of Moldova 
World Economy in 2003 
The faster pace of the world economy upturn in 2003 as compared to 
the previous year was largely due to economic outcomes in North 
America, Asia and, to a smaller extent in Europe. 
The acceleration of economic growth was determined by a number of 
factors: geopolitical stability, stabilization of petroleum prices, 
improvement of confidence indicators, etc. As in accordance with 
estimates, global production output in 2003 increased by 3.2% as 
compared to 3.0% in 2002. 
The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in the USA rose by 3.1%, being 
supported by public authorities through monetary and fiscal policy 
incentives. The high deficit of the federal budget and the current 
account of the balance of payments on the background of low interest 
rates and reduced demand of foreign investors for American assets 
had caused in 2003 the depreciation of US$ against main world 
currencies. Average annual inflation grew slightly to 2.3% from 1.6% 
in 2002. Unemployment rate dropped down to 5.7% as of December 
2003.       
The economic growth in the Euro zone denoted a moderate pace, 
accounting for 0.4%, due to both internal reasons and the evolution of 
the exchange rate of the common currency against US$. Average 
annual inflation supported the price stability trend in the Euro zone, 
accounting for an estimate of 2.1% in 2003 compared to 2.3% in 
2002.  At the same time, unemployment increased from 8.4% in 2002 
up to 8.8% in 2003. However, the economies of the largest European 
countries – Germany and France – did not experience economic 
growth in 2003, staying at the level of the previous year. 
Japan, as a result of improved world economic environment in 2003, 
succeeded to reach an economic growth of 2.6%, which is by 3.0 p.p. 
more than in 2002.  
 Countries of Central and Eastern Europe, despite modest economic 
results as compared to 2002, continued to exhibit GDP growth rates 
superior to those achieved in Eurozone members. Within this context 
GDP growth was as follows: in Romania – 4.9%; in Hungary – 2.9%; 
in Poland – 3.7%.  
In Russia, due to favorable external environment, economic growth 
accounted for 7.3%, exceeding by 2.6 p.p. the level of the previous 
year. Inflation slowed to 12.0% in 2003 as compared to 15.1% in 
2002.

In Ukraine economic growth is estimated to account for 9.3%.

On February 4, 2003 Fitch IBCA improved the country rating of the 
Republic of Moldova with regard to long-term external commitments 
from “DD” to “B-“ and reconfirmed the “C” rating for short-term 
credits in foreign currency. Thus, the Republic of Moldova was 
excluded from the list of “default countries”. However, despite the 
improved country rating following the restructuring of Eurobonds in 
2002, Fitch IBCA noted that external debt was still a difficult issue for 
the Republic of Moldova. In May 2003 the international financial 
evaluation Agency Moody’s Investors Service raised the long- and 
medium-term external liabilities rating of the country from “Ca” to 
“Caa1”. In December 2003 Fitch IBCA reconfirmed Moldova’s “B”- 
and “B” positions for long- and short-term external commitments 
ratings, respectively, as well as “B” position for long-term national 
currency rating. The rating perspective of the Republic of Moldova 
was evaluated as stable.

Evolution of Prices and Public Living Standard  
The inflation rate of 15.7% recorded at the end of 2003 reached the 
highest level in the last three years. During the year, inflation denoted 
an average monthly rate of 1.2%, being less influenced by seasonal 
factors as in the previous year. Prices of foodstuffs increased by 
20.0%, exceeding the general inflation rate. At the same time, the 
pick-up in prices for non-food products and services rendered to the 
public accounted for 11.5% and 12.6% respectively (chart no. 13).

higher prices for the main products included 
in the consumer goods basket. The most 
significant rises in foodstuff prices were 
recorded for: wheat flour – by 2.5 times; eggs 
– by 54.9%; fresh fruits – by 48.2%; bread – 
by 37.4%; and sugar – by 18.7%.  
Increases in prices for the majority of non-
food products stayed below the average set in 
this group, except for two types of goods – 
ready-made clothes and footwear, which 
denoted price rises of 16.3% and 15.3% 
respectively.  
The 12.6%-growth in service prices and tariffs was below the general 
inflation rate, except in: telecommunication - by 27.5%; gas

2. Activity of the National Bank of Moldova  
 
Monetary Policy Implementation in 2003 
Monetary Policy Objectives and Results  
Within the framework of the state economic policy, the National Bank 
of Moldova works out and implements the Monetary and Foreign 
Exchange Policy, which is targeted to maintain the stability of the 
national currency by establishing money, credit and exchange market 
conditions based on market economy principles.  
The Monetary and Foreign Exchange Policy for 2003 was subject to 
co-ordination with the state relevant economic and financial 
authorities and approved by the Council of Administration of the 
National Bank of Moldova on December 12, 2002. 
The macroeconomic framework applied for the elaboration of the 
Monetary and Foreign Exchange Policy of the National Bank of 
Moldova for 2003 included: the Forecast on the Economic 
Development of the Republic of Moldova for the years 2003 – 2005 
and the memorandum on economic and financial policies concluded 
between the Government of the Republic of Moldova, the National 
Bank of Moldova and international financial organisations. The basic 
objectives of thereof provided as follows:  
• annual inflation rate of 6.0%; 
• real GDP growth by 6.0% for 2003; 
• state budget deficit of 0.8% of GDP or 201.0 million lei 
(budget deficit to be financed from: internal sources - revenues 
from sales of state securities; external sources; as well as 
privatisation proceeds);  
• average estimated exchange rate for 2003 of MDL 1 for US$ 
14.6;  
• consolidation of international foreign exchange reserves at a 
level sufficient to cover 3 import-months at least.  
The monetary indicators were determined based on the upward trend 
of money demand evolution at a level in line with projected economic 
growth. Within this context, the increase in money supply M3 was 
projected at 23.1% in conditions of the decrease of money velocity to 
3.14. Monetary base was projected in an amount of 3819.6 million lei, 
with an increase by 17.1% as compared to the end of 2002. With the 
view to ensuring due preconditions for a sustainable economic growth, 
crediting of the national economy was projected to raise by 25.7% as 
compared to the end of 2002.

Monetary Policy Instruments  
With the view to reaching the main set objective – to achieve and 
maintain the stability of the national currency, the National Bank of 
Moldova conducted over the reported year a number of measures 
aimed at steering money supply by means of manipulating liquidity in 
the banking sector.  
Banking liquidity was managed in 2003 within the framework of 
existent money market conditions, through both sterilisation 
instruments and provision of additional liquidity to commercial banks.  
Seasonal factors had a significant impact upon liquidity condition in 
the money market. These factors determined the reduction of excess 
reserves during spring and autumn (months of extensive crediting) and 
banks’ tendency to hold excess reserves during months of income tax 
payment, etc. The structural liquidity excess noted at the beginning of 
2003 was caused by the maintenance of means of the state social 
insurance budget.  
Tensions observed in the foreign exchange market in the 1st quarter of 
the year followed by an accelerated depreciation of MDL against US$ 
within this time framework provided the implementation of a series of 
restrictive measures, including the increase of required reserves and of 
the NBM base rate.

Interest Rate Policy  
In conditions when the intermediate objective of the National Bank 
provides steering of money supply, the interest rate policy within the 
set of monetary regulation instruments of the National Bank of 
Moldova played an important role in the context of monetary policy 
implementation.  
The National Bank aimed at orienting interest rates in the money 
market through application of the interest rate corridor  (where the rate 
on overnight credits constituted the upper limit and the rate on deposit 
operations – the lowest limit), thus contributing to the reduction of 
interest rate volatility. The rate on 2-month SS selling REPOs, which 
is the NBM base rate, served as reference level in establishing interest 
rates of NBM monetary instruments.        
To exercise a more efficient control over interest rates in the money 
market, the National Bank of Moldova implemented in September 
2003 a new instrument of banking liquidity management – the 
overnight deposit. The interest rate on this instrument is set as the 
lowest limit of the interest rate corridor.  
With the view to preventing the worsening of inflationary pressures, 
in March 2003 and for the first time in the last two years the National 
Bank of Moldova increased the base rate, aiming at impeding 
excessive rise in domestic demand and at stimulating savings in 
national currency.  
Inflation increase over the level projected for 2003 implied the 
maintenance of restrictive interest rate policy. Within this context, the 
Council of Administration of the National Bank of Moldova decided

Exchange Rate Trends in 2003 
The official exchange rate of the national currency appreciated over 
the year by 4.36% from 13.8220 lei 
per US$ 1 as of December 31, 2002 to 
13.2200 lei per US$ 1 as of December 
31, 2003. The official exchange rate 
evolution 
in 
2003 
denoted 
a 
depreciation trend of the national 
currency against US$ in the period of 
January to April∗ followed by a 
further continuous appreciation by 
10.6% to the end of 2003.  
The main factors that affected the 
exchange rate dynamics over 2003 includes as follows:  
• 
considerable increase of the commercial balance deficit 
conditioned by risen prices for imported goods and services. The 2.7-
time increase of the negative balance of external trade payments in 
the 1st quarter of 2003 as against the same period of 2002 generated a 
more pronounced depreciation of the national currency;  
• 
excessive supply of foreign currency from individuals in the 
2nd semester of 2003 (net value of foreign currency supply from 
individuals∗∗ amounted to US$ 264.1 million, increasing by 67.4% 
from the relevant period of 2002);  
• 
significant appreciation by 20.0% of Euro against US$ in the 
international market over 2003. US$ depreciation against both 
Moldovan leu and Euro determined the enlargement of US$ supply in 
the market following the conversion in lei (and/or Euro) of a part of 
US$ savings.  
During the period of national currency depreciation, the National 
Bank of Moldova conducted a series of measures aimed at redressing 
the condition of the foreign exchange market, including: 1) the 
reduction of the open foreign exchange position for each foreign 
currency from ±10% to ± 5% and of the position for all currencies 
from ± 20% to ± 10%; 2) the decrease of the maintenance term (from 
30 days to 7 days) in the account of resident legal entity of the foreign 
currency purchased in the domestic forex market. In September 2003,

Foreign Exchange Regulation in 2003 
Normative acts approved by the National Bank of Moldova in 2003 
have been aimed at further improving the legal framework in the field 
of foreign exchange regulation. The NBM conducted relevant actions 
to ensure the stability of the national currency and of the domestic 
foreign exchange market, to gradually liberalise foreign exchange 
requirements, as well as to consolidate the foreign exchange control.   
Paying due consideration to pressures occured on the foreign 
exchange market of the Republic of Moldova in the 1st quarter of the 
year, the National Bank approved in April 2003 a series of temporary 
measures aimed at mitigating the negative impact upon the forex 
market and, ultimately, upon the exchange rate of the national 
currency. Those measures included the increase of required reserves 
ratio; the reduction of banks’ open foreign exchange position; as well 
as the decrease of the maintenance term (from 30 banking days to 7 
banking days) in the resident legal entities’ accounts (except banks) 
of the foreign currency purchased in the domestic foreign exchange 
market. Accordingly, the NBM modified the mechanism of foreign 
currency acquisition by resident legal entities.  
In September 2003, considering the MDL exchange rate dynamics in 
the market, the NBM re-established the open foreign exchange 
position limits at the previous level. With regard to the maintenance 
term established for foreign exchange purchased by resident legal 
entities in the domestic foreign exchange market, the period of 7 
banking days was considered sufficient for using according to its 
destination. However, tasking into account the specific character of 
operations through cards, a term exceeding 7 banking days was set 
up.  
In 2003 the National Bank of Moldova liberalised / simplified a 
number of forex rules, including as follows.  
• Non-resident individuals have been allowed, upon leaving the 
Republic of Moldova, to export foreign currency in cash and/or 
traveller’s checks in an amount not exceeding US$ 1000 (relevant 
equivalent) per person, without providing to customs offices of the 
Republic of Moldova of confirming documents;  
• With the view to implementing the provisions of the Bilateral 
Cooperation Protocol signed by the National Bank of Moldova and 
the National Bank of Romania on 16.04.2003 in Bucharest, Romania, 
the NBM increased the ceiling set upon import/export of national 
currency by individuals into/from the Republic of Moldova from 
2500 Moldovan lei to 7000 Moldovan lei. The increased limit shall be 
applied for individuals, both residents and nonresidents.

New Prudential Regulations 
In 2003 the National Bank of Moldova undertook a series of measures 
aimed at maintaining the stability of the banking sector as well as at 
promoting further banking consolidation. Within this scope, the NBM 
revised prudential regulation paying due consideration to the existent 
normative framework and best general standards of banking 
supervision.  
The bank’s capital is the cornerstone of the banking activity, 
representing the source of possible loss coverage and confidence 
establishment. Considering that, the NBM has further put forth effort 
to consolidate the stability of Moldovan banks by increasing 
requirements towards minimum capital. The new required capital was 
raised as to make commercial banks conducting international 
operations be competitive in the world market and hold a capital in 
line with requirements applied in the majority of European states. 
Thus, minimum required capital rose from 32.0 million lei to 50.0 
million lei. Banks operating in the market as of the date of the new 
requirement enforcement shall increase their capital gradually over the 
period of 30.06.2004 to 31.12.2005 from 40.0 million lei up to 50.0 
million lei, depending on held license.  
Special attention in the context of banking sector efficient functioning 
was paid to internal control systems within banks. Under this scope, 
the NBM conducted a series of actions related to the improvement of 
internal control systems of commercial banks. The National Bank 
established requirements on responsibilities to be assumed by bank’s 
management and executive body. Thus, the board of the bank shall 
bear responsibility for the approval and revision, at least once per 
year, of an adequate internal control system, as well as for the 
approval of new internal regulations covering all aspects of the bank’s

reserve requirements of the NBM, in compliance with its prudential requirements.   
 
The mandatory reserves are determined based on the average daily balances of deposit 
accounts and other similar liabilities of the commercial banks, specified for that reason. 
 
Capital and reserves 
 
Both Statutory Fund and reserves are disclosed in the balance sheet at nominal value. 
 
Income taxes 
 
The National Bank of Moldova is not subject to any income or profit taxes.  
 
Interest income and expense 
 
Interest income and expenses are recognised in the profit and loss account on an accrual 
basis.  
 
The recognition of interest income related to credits granted to banks ceases when the 
payment of interest or principal is doubtful, and does so automatically if the principal or 
interest payments are 60 days or more in arrears.  Any interest previously accrued but not 
received on a loan subsequently placed on a non-accruals basis is reversed.  Subsequently, 
the interest is included in income only when received.  Loans are again restated on an 
accruals basis only when the doubt about collectability is removed and when the 
outstanding arrears of interest and principal are received.

Revaluation of foreign currency assets and liabilities  
 
Unrealised gains/losses from foreign exchange currency position represents the daily 
revaluation of the foreign currency denominated assets and liabilities held by the NBM. By 
virtue of its activities as a central bank for currency market intervention purposes, NBM 
maintains open currency positions at reporting dates.  
 
Management of NBM consider that, in line with international guidance, distribution of 
unrealised gains would be an inflationary emission of currency, therefore the unrealized 
portion of foreign revaluation exchange gains being reflected in the income statement, is 
transferred at year-end to a special reserve account against which future unrealized losses 
shall be charged.   
 
Fiduciary activities 
 
The National Bank of Moldova in certain specific instances acts as fiscal agent of the State. 
Assets and income arising from these activities are excluded from these financial 
statements where the National Bank of Moldova acts in fiduciary capacity such as nominee 
or agent. 
 
Pension obligations 
 
The National Bank of Moldova, in the normal course of business, makes payments to the 
Republic of Moldova state pension fund on behalf of its employees for pension, health 
care, social insurance and employment benefits.  The National Bank of Moldova’s 
contributions are expensed as incurred. 
 
Fair value of financial instruments 
 
Fair value is the amount at which a financial instrument could be exchanged in a current 
transaction between willing parties, other than in a forced sale or liquidation, and is best 
evidenced by a quoted market price. 
 
The National Bank of Moldova’s short term assets are carried on the financial statements at 
cost which approximates their fair value because these instruments have short maturity 
terms and are convertible into cash and are settled without significant transaction costs.  
The National Bank of Moldova’s loans and advances are reported at nominal value less an 
estimate for impairment.  The National Bank of Moldova’s foreign securities are classified 
as “available for sale” and reported at fair value.  These items have predominantly short 
repricing terms and carry interest rates which reflect current market conditions.

RISK MANAGEMENT 
 
Through its normal operations, the National Bank of Moldova is exposed to a number of 
risks, the most significant of which are currency, operating, credit, interest rate, and 
liquidity risks.  The structure of the NBM’s assets and liabilities is primarily determined by 
the nature of the National Bank of Moldova’s statutory functions, rather than commercial 
considerations.  At the same time the NBM continually manages its exposure to risk, 
through a variety of risk management techniques.  Risk management of the National Bank 
of Moldova is regulated by its internal instructions, and is closely monitored by the Board 
of Directors of the National Bank of Moldova, as well as Committees and Commissions of 
the National Bank of Moldova.  The Board of Directors, Committees, Commissions and 
related workgroups consider matters related to the monetary, investment and foreign 
exchange policy of the National Bank of Moldova, and set limits for volumes of 
operations, as well as the requirements for assessing counterparties of the National Bank of 
Moldova.  
 
Operating risk is the risk of loss arising from the breakdown of internal controls.  The 
National Bank of Moldova manages this risk through the Internal Audit Department of the 
National Bank of Moldova, which exercises control over accounting policies and 
procedures and the effective functioning of the system of internal controls of the NBM.  
Operating risk relating to operations with currency reserves is controlled by a number of 
internal instructions of the National Bank of Moldova. Segregation of duties between the 
different departments of the National Bank of Moldova (front-office, middle-office and 
back-office) is also considered as one of the mechanisms of managing operating risk. 
 
Interest rate risk 
 
The National Bank of Moldova is exposed to interest rate price risk in domestic and 
foreign markets, principally as a result of investing international foreign exchange 
reserves, lending to Government and other banks, at fixed interest rates, in amounts and for 
periods which differ from those of term deposits and other borrowed funds. 
Furthermore as the National Bank of Moldova’s main objective is to reach monetary 
stability, it may happen that the return on interest represents no priority of the monetary 
policies.   
 
Liquidity risk 
 
Liquidity risk is defined as the risk of loss arising due to the mismatch of the maturities of 
assets and liabilities. The maturities of assets and liabilities and the ability to replace, at an 
acceptable cost, interest-bearing liabilities as they mature, are important factors in 
assessing the liquidity of the National Bank of Moldova and its exposure to changes in 
interest and exchange rates.

Securities issued by Government of the Republic of Moldova and held in the National 
Bank of Moldova’s portfolio have a contractual maturity not longer than 91 days.  
However, at maturity, those securities are rolled over, being repurchased by the 
Government at current market rates and new securities are issued and purchased by the 
National Bank of Moldova. 
 
Loans granted to Government which also have a contractual maturity not longer than 
91 days are also rolled over and accordingly have a longer term duration. At maturity, 
interest accrued is however paid to the National Bank of Moldova. 
 
Credit risk 
 
The National Bank of Moldova takes on exposure to credit risk which is the risk that a 
counterparty will be unable to pay amounts in full when due. The NBM’s maximum 
exposure to credit risk, excluding the value of collateral, is generally reflected in the 
carrying value of financial assets. The impact of possible netting of assets and liabilities to 
reduce potential credit exposure is not significant. 
 
Management of credit risk. Credit risk on transactions with currency reserves is managed 
through the approval of transactions and placements of funds, the establishment of limits 
restricting risk and constant monitoring of positions. Counterparty limits are set based on 
credit ratings and are subject to regular review. Credit risk in the local currency portfolio is 
also monitored and managed. 
 
Currency risk 
 
Currency risk is the risk that the value of financial instruments will fluctuate due to 
changes in foreign exchange rates. 
 
 
Currency Reserves Management 
 
In accordance with the NBM’s law, the tasks of maintaining the safety and liquidity of 
reserve assets, as well as the profitability of reserve assets management transactions, are 
achieved through active diversification of investment by entering into transactions in 
international capital and money markets. Analysis of risks, applied by the National Bank of 
Moldova in the process of managing its currency reserves is maintained by comparing 
factual risks level with set limits. Credit risks are additionally limited by the minimum 
acceptable credit ratings and operational limits.

Market risk
