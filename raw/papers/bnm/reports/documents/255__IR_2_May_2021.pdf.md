Inﬂation Report, May 2021 (National Bank of Moldova)
Pursuant to the provisions of art. 4 para. (1) of Law no. 548/1995 on the National Bank of Moldova
(republished in the Ofﬁcial Gazette of the Republic of Moldova, 2015, no. 297-300, art. 544, with
subsequent amendments) the fundamental objective of the National Bank of Moldova is to ensure and
maintain price stability.
This objective is achieved in the light of the monetary policy framework associated with the direct
inﬂation targeting regime. The quantitative target is to maintain inﬂation, measured by the consumer
price index at 5.0% per year with a possible deviation of ±1.5 percentage points.
National Bank of Moldova appreciates the role of transparency and predictability of monetary policy in
strengthening institutional credibility and ensuring inﬂation target.
In this context, the Inﬂation
Report is the main communication tool, which reﬂects the latest analyzes of the situation in the
internal and external environment, the evolution of the inﬂation process and economic activity, along
with aspects related to the conduct of monetary policy.
At the same time, the Report incorporates the inﬂation forecast for the eight-quarter horizon, as well
as the risks and uncertainties associated with this projection.
Summaries of the minutes of the meetings of the NBM Executive Board on the promotion of monetary
policy are part of the Report, which are published six months after the decision in accordance with the
NBM’s medium-term monetary policy strategy (approved by the Decision of the Executive Board of
the National Bank of Moldova no. 303 of December 27, 2012).
According to art.
69 para.
(2) the Inﬂation Report shall be submitted to Parliament and the
Government within 45 days of the end of the quarter.
The Inﬂation Report no.
2, May 2021 was discussed and approved at the meeting of the NBM
Executive Board on April 30, 2021.

Summary
Following the downward dynamics of the previous year, in the ﬁrst quarter of 2021, the annual inﬂation
rate marked the beginning of an upward trajectory. Thus, increasing from 0.2% in January 2021 to 1.5%
in March 2021, still being placed below the lower limit of the range of 5.0% ± 1.5 percentage points.
The increase in the annual inﬂation rate in the ﬁrst three months of the year was mainly supported by
the rise in oil prices, the depreciation of the exchange rate and the adjustment of excise duties which
led to an acceleration in fuel prices in the domestic market. At the same time, the modest harvest
for certain crops from the previous year, the increase in import prices and the intensiﬁcation of cost
pressures led to an increase in food prices on the local market.
At the same time, disinﬂationary pressures, especially on core inﬂation, continued to be exerted by
negative domestic demand in the context of the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on the domestic
economy. A disinﬂationary impact on the annual inﬂation rate was exerted by the decrease in the annual
rate of regulated prices as a result of the adjustment of the electricity tariff from February 2021. In the
next period, the upward trajectory of the annual CPI rate will continue, mainly due to cost pressures. In
this sense, the dynamics of prices in industry outlined a sharp upward trajectory at the beginning of this
year. At the same time, the subsequent dynamics of the CPI remain marked by the uncertainty of the
timing and magnitude of some tariff adjustments, but also by the impact of weather conditions on food
prices on the domestic market.
The beginning of 2021 was highlighted in the world economy by increasing the prospects for the
vaccination process against COVID-19, which is more advanced in developed economies, but is practically
at the beginning in poorer economies that depend on vaccine donations through the COVAX platform. It
also determines growth prospects by country and region, the IMF said in its new report1 that, economic
growth will be very fragmented worldwide in 2021. In February-March, the third pandemic wave was
registered in the European Union, which led to the tightening of quarantine restrictions. At the same
time, the European Union lags behind the advanced savings in vaccination, following the problems of
delivery and redistribution of vaccines.
Rising economic outlook has boosted markets, leading to rising commodity and food prices, production,
warehousing and transport costs. Thus, in most economies there has been an increase in inﬂationary
pressures related to costs, even if consumer demand continues to be relatively weak. Brent oil prices
rose to more than 65 USD as OPEC+ controlled oil supply in line with demand developments since May
2020. At the same time, international quotations for food products increased in March 2021 by 24.6%
on an annual basis, reﬂecting a reduced supply generated by poor harvests.
In the region, the appreciation trend of the Russian ruble as a result of rising oil prices has been ﬂattened
by the tightening of the geopolitical fund and the announcement of new sanctions. The Turkish lira also
depreciated signiﬁcantly after a new dismissal of the central bank governor that increased investor
distrust in the independence of the monetary authority.
In the context of internal restrictions related to COVID-19, the regional economic climate affected by
the pandemic, as well as the adverse conditions for the agricultural sector in the fourth quarter of
2020, the dynamics of economic activity continued to be negative after the sharp decline in previous
periods. Thus, in the fourth quarter of 2020, GDP was 3.3% lower than in the same period of 2019,
the respective dynamics being, however, higher than anticipated in the Inﬂation Report no.1, 2021.
Therefore, the effect of aggregate demand on prices continued to be negative. At the same time, the
seasonally adjusted series reﬂects a 2.6% increase in GDP compared to the third quarter of 2020. At the
same time, along with the agricultural sector, trade and transport registered signiﬁcant decreases that
marked the dynamics of GDP. Construction and the ﬁnancial sector continued to evolve positively. From
the perspective of uses, domestic consumption continued to register a negative dynamic, but it was less
pronounced compared to the third quarter of 2020, against the background of the negative dynamics of

disposable income of the population in 2020, but also the uncertainty about further developments of
the economic situation. A minor positive impact was determined by the investment dynamics. Exports,
in the context of a modest external demand, of some restrictions related to COVID-19 in the region, but
also of a smaller harvest, continued the negative dynamics from the beginning of 2020. The decrease in
domestic demand also led to a negative dynamics of imports in the fourth quarter of 2020. The decline
in economic activity against the background of the impact of the restrictions related to COVID-19 led
to the continuation of the negative evolution of the economically active population and the employed
population. Most of the operational indicators on the dynamics of economic activity since the beginning
of this year continued to register a negative dynamics, outlining preconditions for a modest evolution of
GDP in the ﬁrst quarter of 2021.
In January-March 2021, the revenues of the national public budget recorded increases, being 11.1%
above the level recorded in the similar period of 2020. National public budget expenditures increased,
being 12.1% above the level in the ﬁrst quarter of 2020. At the same time, on the market of state
securities allocated during the ﬁrst quarter of 2021, interest rates decreased on all types of maturities,
except for 2-year government bonds.
In the ﬁrst quarter of 2021, excessive liquidity amounted to 6.3 billion lei, decreasing compared to the
previous quarter by 0.1 billion lei.
The monetary aggregates in the ﬁrst quarter of 2021 registered a positive dynamics in annual terms,
largely determined by the change in the "money in circulation" component and the volume of deposits
in the national currency.
During the ﬁrst quarter of 2021, two meetings of the Executive Board of the National Bank of Moldova on
monetary policy decisions took place. Following the assessment of the balance of internal and external
risks and the short- and medium-term inﬂation outlook, the Executive Board of the National Bank of
Moldova, at its meetings of January 29, 2021 and March 5, 2021, decided to maintain the base rate
applied to major monetary policy operations at the level of 2.65% per year (level set on November 6,
2020).
The average interest rates on the balance on loans in national currency increased by 0.1 percentage
points, and on deposits - decreases. Average interest rates on loans and foreign currency deposits
continued their downward trend.
As in the previous report, the forecasts for the evolution of the external environment show a gradual
recovery of the world economy, in conditions of uncertainty, but without major risks. Both in the euro
area and in the Russian Federation, economic growth is expected to be lower in 2021 and higher in 2022.
At the same time, external pro-inﬂationary pressures will be more pronounced this year and lighter next
year. In the current forecast round, currency projections have been adjusted for the appreciation of the
US dollar and the depreciation of the euro and the Russian ruble. Projected increases in oil and food
prices on foreign markets are signiﬁcantly more pronounced in the current forecast round.
In the second quarter of this year, the annual inﬂation rate will reverse its trajectory and show an
upward trend, reaching the inﬂation target at the end of the forecast horizon. During the ﬁrst two
consecutive quarters it will continue to position itself below the lower limit of the variation interval, but
starting with the fourth quarter of 2021 it will return to the interval, being close to the target until the
end of the forecast period.
The annual rate of core inﬂation will change in the ﬁrst quarter of the forecast, thus increasing towards
the end of the forecast horizon under the inﬂuence of the recovery of aggregate demand and the side
effects generated by rising fuel prices. The annual rate of food prices will start a slight upward trend
until the beginning of next year, after which it will vary slightly for the rest of the forecast period. The
annual rate of regulated prices will increase sharply from the second half of the current year to the
middle of next year, after which it will stabilize by the end of the forecast period. The annual rate of fuel
prices will increase signiﬁcantly in the ﬁrst quarter. Subsequently, in the ﬁrst half of next year, it will
decrease rapidly until the second half of next year, increasing insigniﬁcantly towards the end of the
forecast horizon.
Aggregate demand will continue to recover, although it will be disinﬂationary over the entire forecast
horizon. The return of domestic aggregate demand will be inﬂuenced by the recovery of external
demand, the improvement of monetary conditions and the recovery in agriculture this year.


Summary
Real monetary conditions will be restrictive during the ﬁrst two consecutive quarters of the forecast
horizon, having a negative impact on aggregate demand, then will be slightly stimulating.
The current forecast for inﬂation, compared to the previous inﬂation report, increased until the ﬁrst
quarter of 2022 and largely decreased for the rest of the comparable period2. The current forecast
for core inﬂation has been revised downwards over the entire comparable period. The annual rate of
food prices has been revised upwards until the ﬁrst quarter of 2022 and downwards until the end of
the comparable period. The current forecast for the annual rate of regulated prices has been revised
downwards until the fourth quarter of 2021 and upwards for the rest of the comparable period. The
forecast for the annual rate of fuel prices has been revised upwards until the ﬁrst quarter of 2021 and
downwards – for the rest of the comparable period.

External sector
• Fragmentation of global economic growth. In its new

in the ﬁeld of retail and HoReCa segment. Poor economies
have limited access to vaccine procurement and depend on

vaccination process in these countries is progressing very

restrictions and respectively the postponement of the
economic recovery of the affected activities (disinﬂationary).
• Stability of oil prices. Due to the fulﬁllment of the OPEC+
plan, oil prices gradually increased and remained stable
in the vicinity of the value of 65 USD/barrel.
Continued
implementation of the OPEC+ plan, a rapid pace of global
vaccination and possible geopolitical risks could increase oil
prices above projected levels. At the same time, the return
of Iranian oil to the world market in case of resumption of
dialogue between the United States and Iran, the worsening
of the pandemic situation in case of a new strain of the virus,
the non-fulﬁllment of the OPEC+ plan and the increase of US
oil production are risks (uncertainty).

in the analytical part of the current report, production costs
related to quotations for raw materials, transport and storage
costs have increased signiﬁcantly in recent months. These
have increased pro-inﬂationary pressures, given that in most
inﬂation-targeting countries monetary authorities have kept
interest rates to a minimum to stimulate domestic demand.
Thus, in the coming months, several economies will face the
need to increase monetary policy rates in order to alleviate
inﬂationary pressures, even if domestic demand continues to
require further stimulus (proinﬂationary).
• The depreciation of the Russian ruble as a result of
the worsening geopolitical situation. In recent weeks,
the US and the EU have introduced new economic sanctions
against the Russian Federation, which has helped increase
the risk premium of the Russian ruble. At the same time, the
Western bloc threatened to tighten sanctions if the situation
continues to worsen. Even if the stability/growth of oil prices

The meeting of the NBM Executive Board (EB) began with the presentation of Mr. Radu Cuhal regarding the
analysis of the latest available information from the external and internal environment, the trends and perspectives
of the main macroeconomic indicators. At the same time, the new forecast round was presented, as well as the
risks and uncertainties associated with the inﬂation process for the next eight quarters.
Appreciating the external macroeconomic situation, EB members noted that recent information reﬂects
improved global economic conditions in the third quarter of 2020, with a partial exit from the crisis generated by
COVID-19, but annual growth rates remain lower than in most countries before the pandemic. At the same time, it
was stressed that uncertainty persists regarding the trajectory of the global economic recovery in the context of
the ampliﬁcation of the second wave of the pandemic in recent weeks.
EB members stressed that amid the consequences of the pandemic, along with monetary policy stimulus
measures taken by most central banks, global inﬂation remains low, a fact speciﬁc to Moldova’s main trading
partners. reﬂects in the dynamics of import prices.
Subsequently, the discussions of the EB members focused on the assessment of the internal macroeconomic
environment. EB members noted that according to NBS data, in the third quarter of 2020, the annual inﬂation rate
continued its downward trajectory since the beginning of this year, registering 2.3% in September 2020 compared
to 4.3% in June 2020. It was found that this development was due to the slowdown in rising food prices and core
inﬂation in the face of disinﬂationary pressures from domestic demand. At the same time, it was observed that the
decrease of regulated prices in the context of the adjustment of the electricity tariff from the end of July 2020, as
well as the seasonal appreciation of the national currency supported the downward trend of annual inﬂation.
In discussions on the economy of the Republic of Moldova, EB members found that it entered negative
territory in the second quarter of 2020, with clear prospects of maintaining in this territory in the next quarter, but
with less pronounced rates, a fact signaled by operational indicators provided by NBS for the period July-September
2020. It was noted that in the second quarter of 2020, gross domestic product contracted by 14.0% in real terms
compared to the same period of the previous year. At the same time, it was emphasized that this dynamic was
caused by the decrease in the annual rate of ﬁnal consumption of households by -17.0% amid the reduction of
disposable income of the population and the associated restrictions COVID-19 on commercial activities and the
postponement of household spending in the context of the uncertainties caused by the pandemic. Attention was
also paid to other components of domestic demand, such as investment, public and private government
consumption in the absence of stimulus measures, with exports pointing out that they also had a negative impact
on economic growth, which was offset by negative import dynamics. At the same time, it was pointed out that the
adverse weather conditions were also associated with the increase in GDP, which considerably reduced the
performance of the agricultural sector.
Analyzing the situation on the labor market, EB members noted that in the second quarter of 2020 the
negative evolution of the economically active and employed population accentuated, being by 9.0% and 8.8%
lower compared to the similar period of the previous year. The salary fund by economy contracted by 1.4% in real
terms, and the average number of employees decreased by 8.2% compared to the second quarter of 2019.
At the same time, the EB members commented on the crediting process in the context of monetary policy

In the discussions that followed, EB members referred to the current forecast, noting that the gradual
recovery of the world economy has led to a partial improvement in forecasts of changes in the external
environment, but external demand will continue to be negative. It turned out that the major change in external
assumptions reﬂects the recent signiﬁcant appreciation of the single European currency and, at the same time, the
depreciation of the Russian ruble. At the same time, it has been pointed out that the insigniﬁcant ﬂuctuation of the
Urals oil forecast is due to the low volatility of oil prices in recent months and the slow growth in consumer demand
for oil.
With reference to the forecasted inﬂation trend, it was mentioned that the annual inﬂation rate will decrease
slightly by the end of this year, and starting with the second quarter of 2021 it will record an upward trajectory,
positioning itself at inﬂation level – target towards the end of the forecast horizon. In the period IV quarter 2020 –
quarter III 2021, annual inﬂation will remain below the lower limit of the range.
At the same time, it was emphasized that the average annual inﬂation will register the level of 4.0% and
2.5% in 2020 and 2021, respectively. Compared to the previous forecast, it was noted that the current forecast of
the average annual inﬂation rate is 0.9 percentage points lower by 2020 and 1.7 percentage points lower by 2021.
Analyzing the balance of risks to inﬂation, EB members outlined the persistence of those of a disinﬂationary
nature. Negative aggregate demand was highlighted, although in partial recovery it will continue to generate
disinﬂationary pressures over the entire forecast horizon (quarter IV 2020 – quarter III 2022). In this context, the
factors that conditioned the sharp decrease in aggregate demand were listed. These are limited to anti-epidemic
measures, the reduction of external demand, the negative ﬁscal impulse, as well as the unfavorable
agro-meteorological conditions that have affected the volume of agricultural production. At the same time, it was
emphasized that maintaining in the following periods the discrepancy of the real effective exchange rate of the
national currency against the currencies of the main trading partners will affect the quality and dynamics of the
economic recovery.
At the same time, EB members noted that recent information suggests an increased degree of uncertainty
about the duration and intensity of the pandemic and the implications for economic recovery. They also referred to
uncertainties associated with agricultural production in 2021, at the time and magnitude of the adjustment of
tariffs to regulated services, along with the proinﬂationary risk associated with the vulnerability of prices to
domestic fruits and vegetables to weather conditions in the near future.
In these circumstances, the Executive Board decided to reduce the base rate and the rates related to the
permanent facilities granted to banks by the NBM by 0.1 percentage points. It was noted that the decision aims to
create the necessary monetary conditions to support aggregate domestic demand, the lending process and the
overall economic support, which will consequently bring inﬂation back within the range of ± 1.5 percentage points
from the inﬂation target 5.0% in the medium term.
During the meeting, the EB of the NBM analyzed and approved the Inﬂation Report no. 4, 2020 – a document
that includes the latest analyzes of the internal and external situation associated with the inﬂationary process,
economic activity, promotion of monetary policy. At the same time, it includes the new forecast, along with the
risks and uncertainties associated with it.
At the end of the meeting, EB members stressed the need for continuous monitoring of the macroeconomic
situation created by the pandemic implications, reiterating that in due course, the NBM, without prejudice to its
fundamental objective of ensuring price stability, will come up with measures to maintain sufﬁcient liquidity within
licensed banks, thus supporting a viable and stable banking system.
Thus, taking as a benchmark the assessments of the latest available macroeconomic information from the
internal and external environment, as well as the assumptions and assumptions taken into account in preparing the
new forecast round, the NBM Executive Board unanimously adopted the following decision:
