SYNTHESIS 
During 2018, the financial system of the 
Republic of Moldova was characterized by a 
high level of resilience and, at the same time, 
by uneven evolutions of the risks it faces. 
Against the background of economic growth 
marked by internal vulnerabilities (increasing 
the balance of payments deficit, volatile 
inflation) and external ones (international 
trade tensions, Brexit), the financial system 
contributes quite modestly to the recovery of 
the economic situation. At the same time, we 
are witnessing the return of the lending 
process from banks on a positive trend. 
Lending to the population (individuals) had 
upward dynamics, the financing being granted 
by both the banking and non-banking sectors. 
Also, during the year the phenomenon of 
mortgage lending grew. 
The banking sector has achieved satisfactory 
financial performance, maintaining strong 
positions of profitability and solvency. Banks 
have maintained an adequate degree of 
capitalization in the transition process to the 
Basel III requirements. During this period there 
were changes in the structure of banking 
shareholders, three banks came with quality 
international shareholders.   
During 2018, banks registered a favorable level 
of liquidity, and the liquidity surplus in the 
banking sector did not increase significantly 
due to the activation of the targeting of liquid 
assets for lending, thus, the previous year 
representing a period of return of banking 
intermediation to trends positive. However, 
the high level of liquidity leads to the 
persistence of complementary risks, therefore, 
maintaining the means available in liquid 
assets to the detriment of lending to the real 
economy has medium-term effects on 
economic growth and could lead to 
materialization of future inflationary risks of 
lending with the direction of the respective 
means in the economy in a short period of 
time. 
The accelerated evolution of the non-bank 
lending sector continued, and the non-bank 
lending of the population grew at a higher rate 
than bank lending and the growth of the 
national economy. This trend creates premises 
for the accumulation of risks in the non-bank 
lending sector and for the increase of the 
general level of indebtedness of the population. 
Credit risk related to foreign currency loans 
remains a major risk that may contribute to 
increasing the exposure of borrowers, to 
weakening their financial situation and, 
respectively, to increasing the risk of non-
repayment of loans in the event of unfavorable 
exchange rate fluctuations. 
The conditions established on the financial 
market indicate the importance of aligning the 
regulatory and supervisory requirements 
applicable to the banking and non-banking 
sectors with international standards and 
practices in the field. In general, the year 2018 
is characterized by numerous achievements in 
the context of strengthening financial stability. 
These include adjusting the regulatory and 
banking supervision framework to Basel III, 
approving the Law on the National Financial 
Stability Committee, improving the bank 
deposit guarantee scheme, and increasing the 
guarantee ceiling to MDL 20,000 today and 
MDL 50,000 in the year 2020 and the approval 
of the Law on Non-Bank Lending 
Organizations.

The main types of risks associated with the cost 
of the state debt of the Republic of Moldova, 
identified as market risks are:  
1) Currency risk; 
2) Refinancing risk; 
3) Interest rate risk. 
Currency risk 
Currency risk is one of the main categories of 
risks associated with the government debt 
portfolio, affecting both its cost and volume. At 
the end of the reporting year, 55.7 percent of the 
government debt portfolio belonged to the 
external government debt (Chart 1.2.5), fully 
denominated in foreign currency. In 2018, the 
share of external government debt in total 
government debt decreased by 0.6 pp against 
the background of increasing domestic 
government debt.  
The structure of government debt by 
currencies, with the breakdown of the SDR 
basket, highlights the vulnerability of the 
government debt portfolio to EUR and USD, 
which implies the need for continuous 
monitoring of currency risk. Thus, the total 
share of government debt in EUR and USD 
represents 45.6 percent of the government debt 
portfolio (Chart 1.2.5).

Domestic and external government debt repayment

The forecast for the domestic government debt 
repayment profile shows the peak of payment 
in 2019, conditioned by the fact that the 
domestic state debt is characterized, mainly, by 
the short repayment term (up to 1 year, 
inclusive). According to the MF data, in 2019 
there will be reimbursed SS with a maturity of 
two years issued in order to create the liquidity 
reserve in March 2017, but also SS with a 
maturity of 3 years. The short-term domestic 
government debt segment to be refinanced each 
year through new SS issues represents a 
significant risk for the state budget, in the 
context of possible increases in interest rates on 
the domestic market. 
The analysis of the external government debt 
shows an increase in the amount of repayment 
payments by 2020, after which a gradual 
decrease is forecast until 2023. Starting with 
2023, the external government debt will have a 
significantly lower degree of volatility.

3. MACROPRUDENTIAL POLICY 
3.1. 
Macroprudential policy objectives 
Financial stability is a precondition for the 
financial system to provide credit services, thus 
ensuring sustainable economic growth. The global 
financial crisis has clearly highlighted the need 
for macro-prudential oversight to prevent and 
reduce systemic risk. 
The concept of "macroprudential policy" is 
gaining popularity after the last world crisis. 
The Regulation on the macro-prudential 
oversight of the financial system and the 
establishment of the European Systemic Risk 
Board (ESRB) responsible for carrying out 
macro-prudential oversight at EU level has 
been adopted at EU level.  
According to the recommendation issued by 
the ESRB15, the goal of macro-prudential policy 
is to contribute to safeguarding the stability of 
the financial system, including by 
strengthening the resilience of the financial 
system and reducing the accumulation of 
systemic risks, thereby ensuring a sustainable 
contribution of the financial sector to 
economic growth. 
To achieve the final objective, 5 intermediate 
objectives are proposed:  
− 
reducing and preventing excessive 
credit growth and leverage;  
− 
reduction and prevention of excessive 
maturity mismatch and illiquidity in the 
market;  
− 
limiting the concentrations of direct 
and indirect exposures;  
− 
limiting the systemic impact of 
misaligned incentives in order to reduce 
moral hazard;  
− 
strengthening the resilience of financial 
infrastructures.  
The achievement of the intermediate objectives 
and, as a result, of the final objective is 
achieved by applying macroprudential 
instruments aimed at preventing or reducing 
the identified risks.

3.3. 
Future macroprudential measures 
The set of macro-prudential instruments 
available to achieve the intermediate objectives of 
the macro-prudential policy foreseen for the next 
period includes measures to limit the excessive 
indebtedness of debtors, as well as liquidity 
requirements. 
NBM data show a significant increase in 
lending to individuals, an evolution with 
potential negative effects both on the financial 
system (through non-performing loans due to 
the relaxation of lending standards) and on 
future economic growth (through the decrease 
in consumption of over-indebted debtors).  
From this perspective, the definition of 
prudential limits that would establish the 
maximum share of loan payments relative to 
the debtor's income (ratio between debt service 
and total income - DSTI) or the degree of 
coverage with collateral (ratio between loans 
and value of collateral - LTV) aims reducing 
vulnerabilities in lending activity. These 
measures aim to prevent over-indebtedness of 
vulnerable debtors and, respectively, to protect 
banks by reducing the risk of default on loans. 
At the same time, in order to reduce the risks 
that may arise from banking, it is important to 
establish an indicator of leverage, which will 
limit the ability of banks to excessively 
increase the volume of assets on account of 
borrowed funds. That instrument is found in 
the Basel III regulatory package and its 
establishment is provided for in Law 202/2017.  
From the perspective of maintaining sufficient 
liquidity to serve banking activities, but also in 
order to ensure a stable financing of the 
activity, the regulatory framework is to be 
completed with two liquidity requirements - 
the requirement of liquidity coverage ratio and 
the requirement of net stable funding ratio. 
These requirements pursue the intermediate 
objective of reducing and preventing excessive 
maturity mismatch and illiquidity in the 
market.

6.1. 
Regulations with implications for

The year 2018 was marked by multiple changes in 
the legislative and normative framework related 
to the financial system. 
In 2018, following the entry into force of Law 
no. 202/2017 on the activity of banks a series of 
normative acts were promoted and adopted. 
Thus, capital requirements have been 
established, considering the risks inherent in 
the banking activity, namely: credit, market, 
operational and settlement/delivery risk. The 
mentioned regulations were elaborated in the 
context of the NBM's commitment to align the 
local standards with the European ones, within 
the obligations assumed by the EU-Moldova 
Association Agreement. Therefore, the 
following normative acts have been adopted: 
Regulation on banks' own funds and capital 
requirements (regulates the methodology for 
calculating own funds and establishes the 
requirements for own funds and the elements 
included in the calculation of own funds, 
deductions from own fund items, reductions in 
own funds levels and other requirements for 
determining them); Regulation on capital 
buffers of banks (establishes requirements 
regarding the own funds that banks must hold 
in order to set up capital buffers, with the 
ultimate goal of preventing and mitigating 
macroprudential risk and / or systemic risk, as 
well as the particularities related to the 
establishment by the NBM and the calculation 
by banks of these capital buffers); Regulation 
on the treatment of credit risk for banks 
according to the standardized approach 
(establishes the calculation elements of the 
exposure weights according to the degree of 
credit risk assumed by the bank); Regulation on 
credit risk mitigation techniques used by banks 
(lays down rules to standardize the 
requirements for credit risk mitigation 
techniques according to the standardized 
approach, the principles for recognition and 
the minimum conditions that any eligible form 
of credit protection must meet in order for its 
effects on credit risk mitigation to be 
recognized); Regulation on the treatment of 
operational risk for banks according to the 
basic approach and the standardized approach 
(lays down the rules on prudential 
requirements that banks must meet in relation 
to their own funds requirements  regarding 
fully quantifiable elements, uniform and 
standardized elements of operational risk in 
accordance with the basic and standardized 
approaches and sets out which are the own 
funds requirements for operational risk for 
those approaches); Regulation on market risk 
treatment according to the standardized 
approach (establishes the methods of 
determining the own funds requirements for 
market risk according to the standardized 
approach for both the trading book and all the 
bank's exposures); Regulation on the treatment 
of settlement / delivery risk for banks  (sets 
requirements for own funds related to 
settlement risk concerning transactions in and 
out of the trading book that have remained 
unresolved after the scheduled delivery date); 
Regulation on the requirements of the 
members of the management body of the bank, 
of the financial holding company or of the 
mixed holding company, the managers of the 
branch of a bank of another state, the persons 
holding key positions and the liquidator of the 
bank in liquidation (contributed to the increase 
of an efficient and prudent administration of

previous provisions related to the

the determination of the categories of persons 
with key positions and requirements towards 
these persons); Regulation on the banking
