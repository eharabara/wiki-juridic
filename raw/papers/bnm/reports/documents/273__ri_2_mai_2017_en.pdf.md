Summary
Inﬂation
In the ﬁrst quarter of 2017, the annual average inﬂation rate
was 4.3 percent, by 1.8 percentage points more compared to
the previous quarter. In March 2017, inﬂation increased up to
the value of 5.1 percent. As a result, this fell within the target
inﬂation range set in the Medium-term monetary policy strategy.
The increase of annual inﬂation rate during the reporting period
was determined mainly by the increase in tariffs for healthcare
services, the adjustment of excise duty for some categories of
products, certain pressures from aggregate demand, increase of
oil prices and by the effect of a low base period of 2016. The
annual rate of core inﬂation also recorded a more pronounced
upward trend, increasing up to 4.6 percent in March 2017. The
annual rate of food prices increased from 2.8 percent in December
2016 to 5.0 percent in March 2017. The fuel prices increased from
1.6 percent in December to 12.5 percent in March 2017. At the
end of the ﬁrst quarter of 2017, the regulated prices increased by
3.6 percent than the same period of the previous year. In the ﬁrst
quarter of 2017, the actual annual inﬂation rate was higher to that
anticipated within the forecasting round presented in the Inﬂation
Report no. 1, 2017, due mainly to the unexpected increase in
tariffs for healthcare services.

Medium-term inﬂation forecasting
According to the current round of forecasting, the output gap will
record positive values in the ﬁrst four quarters of the forecasting
horizon and negative values in the other next quarters.
The
placement of the economic activity above its potential level, on
medium-term, will cause disinﬂationary pressures, while the
negative values of output gap during the last four quarters of the
forecasting horizon will generate disinﬂationary pressures. The
real monetary conditions will have a heterogeneous impact on the
demand. The real effective exchange rate will manifest incentive
effects in the ﬁrst quarter of the forecasting period, after which it
will produce restrictive monetary conditions for the rest of the
forecasting horizon. The real interest rate will be incentive for the
entire forecasting horizon,
except the last two forecasting
quarters when it will be insigniﬁcant restrictive.
According to the current projection, the annual average CPI
inﬂation rate will record an upward trend until the fourth quarter
of 2017,
after which it will decrease until the end of the
forecasting horizon. Starting with the second quarter of 2017, the
annual inﬂation rate will be above the range of variation during
four consecutive quarters.
Thereafter, this will fall within the
target inﬂation range by the end of the forecasting horizon. This
will record the maximum level of 8.1 percent in the fourth quarter
of 2017, while the minimum level of 4.1 percent in the fourth
quarter of 2018. Compared to the previous forecast, the average
annual values of the inﬂation rate are higher by 1.6 percentage
points in 2017 and by 0.2 percentage points in 2018.

Box 1 
 
Recent adjustments to healthcare service tariffs and their impact on CPI 
Setting healthcare service tariffs is of a certain specific nature, compared with other authority regulated 
service tariffs. On one hand, they are strictly regulated by tariffs approved by the Ministry of Health, public 
healthcare facilities that operate under self-funding principle within the compulsory healthcare insurance, 
non-profit facilities and are directly contracted by the National Healthcare Insurance Company (NHIC)1. This 
fact contributed to listing healthcare services in the regulated prices structure within the CPI price index 
group components2. On the other hand, many healthcare services are provided in the private sector 
(especially specialized outpatient healthcare assistance, diagnosis laboratories, seldom primary and in-patient 
healthcare assistance) and thus are not under the eye of NHIC in what concerns expenditures. 
The healthcare service tariffs provided both by public and private facilities are influenced by several factors, 
among which the national currency exchange rate, wage expenditures, seasonal factor etc. Still, the 
healthcare services tariffs provided by public facilities are adjusted according to the existing regulations, 
which occurs seldom. According to the law, the healthcare service tariffs are adjusted once in two years3, 
depending on the last consumer price index and annual macroeconomic forecast of the Ministry of Economy 
on inflation forecast, and, if required, if the annual expected norm is exceeded, indexing is additionally carried 
out, starting at 3 percent4 threshold.

At the same time, private sector tariffs for healthcare services are constantly fluctuating, depending on the 
aforementioned factors. Due to this, one can note ongoing fluctuations of services included in health 
component within CPI. They are generated by tariff adjustments in the private sector (Chart no. 1). Ongoing 
adjustments, although minor, of service tariffs provided by private facilities made, in time, a significant gap 
between public and private facilities’ healthcare service tariffs. 
Given that they are spread on a broader timeframe, monthly variations of healthcare subcomponent within 
CPI, generated by private sector evolutions, are significantly lower than their dynamic generated by the

Excise duty growth’s impact on CPI 
The modification of prices for some categories of goods included in the CPI is driven by the adjustment of 
some government Fiscal Policy provisions. Thus, the increase of excise duty quota, which according to the 
Fiscal Code are some state taxes established for some consumer goods, has a direct impact on the inflation.  
According to Art.122 of the Fiscal Code, two categories of excise duties are set for excise goods: in absolute 
value to the measuring unit of good and/or ad valorem in percent from the good’s value. The amount and type 
of excise duty quotas, depending on the type of goods, are shown in Appendices no. 1 and 2 to Title IV of the 
Fiscal Code1. 
Excise duty quotas for some goods are reviewed from time to time both to boost state budget accruals and to 
promote its certain policies. Thus, the growth of excise duty quotas for oil products on the territory of the 
Republic of Moldova is carried out based on gradually bringing the excise duty quotas in line with the quotas 
set by the EU legislation, according to the schedule established in the RM-EU Association Agreement2. Thus, 
under European directives, the minimal excise duty quota for petrol makes up EUR 359 per 1000 l, for diesel 
fuel – EUR 330 per 1000 l and for liquefied gas – EUR 125 per 1000 kg3. 
The increase of excise duty quota for filter and non-filter cigarettes is similarly explained. This quota will 
gradually grow so that it lifts up to the level recorded by the countries in the region, including the European 
standards. Thus, according to the commitments assumed by the Republic of Moldova on bringing the 
structure of excise duty rates applied on processed tobacco in line with the EU directives, the Republic of 
Moldova will apply a quota of no less than EUR 90 per 1000 cigarettes by 2025. 
Besides aligning to the EU standards and accruing new financial resources to the budget, the excise duty 
adjustment can also trigger other beneficial objectives, such as cut of tobacco products consumption, as they 
are products with a negative effect on people’s health and society development in general4.

Analysis of U.S. dollar development under the Federal Reserve System’s monetary policy 
From the second half of 2014, world economy is under a significant and ongoing appreciation of the U.S. dollar 
against other currencies of international circulation. This phenomenon is backed by the Federal Reserve 
System’s (Fed) monetary policy conduct that opposes relevant monetary policies on other currencies of 
international circulation. Fed has three main goals – workforce occupation to the highest degree, stable 
prices and long-term moderate interest rates in the U.S. economy. The Great recession in the U.S. economy 
that triggered the increase of unemployment rate up to 10.0 percent (in October 2009) and the odd increase 
of prices (annual inflation rate of 5.6 percent in July 2008, when the historical average and the targeted level is 
of 2.0 percent), pushed Fed to take unprecedented incentive monetary policy measures. Thus in 2008-2014, 
Fed implemented three consecutive programs of quantitative easing that assumed the purchase of financial 
assets. This cut the value of the U.S. dollar, recording an alternating depreciation against other currencies of 
international circulation. 
In October 2014, Fed completed the last program and conditioned the increase of interest rate range under 
monetary policy goals and economic conditions. Thus, while waiting for interest rate increase, the financial 
markets anticipated the effects, thus conditioning the gradual appreciation of the U.S. dollar, even if Fed has 
sufficiently delayed the increase of interest rate, although the European Central Bank (the European single 
currency being the second most traded currency) gradually reduced the monetary policy rate to 0.0 percent 
and started an asset purchase program similar to Fed quantitative easing programs. Interest rates were 
increased the first time on 15 December 2015, when Fed increased the interest rate range up to 0.25-0.5 
percent. The U.S. dollar appreciation amid this event amplified in 2016 with the predictions on a new increase 
of interest rate range. However, only one year later, on 14 December 2016 Fed did increase the interest rate 
range again by 0.25 percentage points, although the unemployment rate reached a minimum of 4.6 percent in 
November 2016 after August 2007 and the annual inflation grew to the target value of 2.0 percent in 
December 2016. The U.S. dollar appreciation related to Fed interest rate range increase in December 2016 a 
triggered the growth of USDX1 index up to 102.21, the highest level since 2003 (Chart no. 1).

Risks and uncertainties
External sector
Risks and uncertainties related to the development of the external
environment are relatively high.
First, U.S. dollar shows an
exacerbated volatility, causing major divergences on the foreign
currency markets. International oil quotations are relatively stable,
but the growing number of operating oil wells in United States of
America can cause failure of OPEC plan to cut oil production.
Moreover, U.S. dollar volatility and oil prices are risk factors for
international raw material quotations. Thus, within the current
forecasting round, the following risks and uncertainties related to
the development of the external environment are identiﬁed:
• Strong volatility of U.S. dollar. On 15 March 2017, the
Federal Reserve System increased the reference range of
interest rate, but, contrary to the economic theory, the
monthly average for March 2017 points at a depreciation of
U.S. dollar compared with most currencies of international
circulation. Although the economic background signals that
the U.S. dollar should record an upward trend, this currency
actually signiﬁcantly alternated in the ﬁrst months of 2017,
increasing the risk related to the current forecasts.
• Ambiguous risks about international oil quotations.
OPEC’s joint efforts with other oil exporting countries
engaged in the agreement to cut oil production could have
been seen in early 2017. They contributed to the increase
and,
later on,
to the stabilization of international oil
quotations. Nevertheless, statistics related to the ongoing
growth of operating oil well number in the United States of
America fuel the risk of further upholding the excess of oil at
the global level and cutting oil quotations. At the same time,
there are very positive predictions on the development of
large oil consumer economies, which might signiﬁcantly
contribute to the oil quotation growth.
• External demand uncertainties.

countries, main trading partners and countries favored by
emigrants for employment, setting up a favorable framework
for external demand growth. However, the practice of recent
years shows that the approach of forecast at the beginning
of the calendar year requires a degree of precaution. Most
likely, gradual appearance of statistics will contribute to
shaping and creating of a clearer view on external demand
growth rates.
