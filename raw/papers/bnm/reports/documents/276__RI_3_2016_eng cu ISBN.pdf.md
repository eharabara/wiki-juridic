Summary
Inﬂation development
In the second quarter of 2016, the annual inﬂation rate was 7.9
percent, by 3.1 percentage points less compared to the previous
quarter.
In June 2016, inﬂation decreased to 7.4 percent.
However, it continued to be above the upper limit of the inﬂation
target set in the Medium-term monetary policy strategy.
The
downward trend was due to modest domestic demand, electricity
tariff revision, increased supply of fruits and vegetables this year,
and the national currency appreciation. The annual rate of core
inﬂation continued to maintain a higher trajectory than that of
overall inﬂation.
Core inﬂation has also recorded a downward
trajectory throughout the second quarter of 2016, decreasing
from 10.2 percent in March to 9.3 percent in June. In June 2016,
the annual growth rate of food and regulated prices slowed to 7.6
and 7.4 percent, respectively. In the second quarter of 2016, oil
prices decreased on average by 3.4 percent compared to the
same period of the previous year. The average actual value of
inﬂation for the second quarter of 2016 was only by 0.2
percentage points lower than the anticipated 8.1 percent in the
forecasting round of Inﬂation Report no. 2, 2016.
External environment
In the second quarter of 2016, there were several events with
signiﬁcant
impact
on
global
economic
development.
An
extraordinary meeting of OPEC with other big oil producers has
held on April 17, 2016, where the discussions on reducing global
oil production were unsuccessful. On June 23, 2016, the UK held a
referendum on its EU membership,
where 51.9 percent of
participants voted in favour of leaving the EU. It should be also
mentioned the resumption of bilateral relations between Turkey
and the Russian Federation in late June and the recent coup in
Turkey, which caused economic instability of the country. These
events have generated signiﬁcant market volatility, increasing
risks to global economic development.
Economic developments
In the ﬁrst quarter of 2016, GDP grew by 0.8 percent compared to
the same period of 2015, the annual growth rate of economic
activity was higher than in the second half of last year. However,
economic growth was modest, so hovering below its potential.
Positive trends in GDP was due to domestic demand, while
exports generated a very negative impact on GDP growth. Thus,

Monetary policy
In the second quarter of 2016, the Executive Board of the National
Bank of Moldova held two meetings on monetary policy decisions.
Following the assessment of internal and external balance of
risks to which the economy of the Republic of Moldova could
be subject and the inﬂation outlook in the short and medium term,
the Executive Board of the NBM decided to decrease the base rate
applied to main monetary policy operations by 2.0 percentage
points within each meeting. Thus, at its meeting of April 28, 2016,
the base rate was lowered from 17.0 (a level set at the meeting of
March 31, 2016) to 15.0 percent annually, at the meeting of May
26, 2016 from 15.0 to 13.0 percent annually.
In the second quarter of 2016, the growth rate of monetary
aggregates was positive, the quarterly average in annual terms
constituted 4.8 percent for M2 (by 7.5 percentage points higher
than in the ﬁrst quarter of 2016) and 0.9 percent for M3 (by 4.4
percentage points higher than the previous quarter).
Reﬂecting the downward trend of the base rate applied on the
main monetary policy operations of the National Bank of Moldova,
the average interest rates on outstanding deposits have reversed
the upward trend, as a result of which the average interest rates
on credits granted by the banking system also recorded a slight
decline.
During the reporting period, as compared to the ﬁrst quarter of
2016, the average annual interest rates on the balance of credits
decreased by 0.08 percentage points on those in national currency
and by 0.18 percentage points on those in foreign currency,
recording values to the level of 13.60 percent and 6.42 percent,
respectively. The average annual interest rate on deposits in MDL
was 14.93 percent, by 0.18 percentage points less compared to
the previous quarter.

Medium-term inﬂation forecasting
The current round of forecasts anticipates a continuation of the
negative dynamics of the output gap for the entire forecasting
horizon. It will recover slowly, but will continue to be negative,
turning positive only towards the end of the forecasting horizon.
Compared to the previous round of forecasting, the estimated
aggregate demand was reconﬁgured.
The evolution of the
economic activity mainly below its potential, on medium-term, will
determine disinﬂationary pressures from domestic demand,
although more moderate compared to the previous forecast.
According to the current projection, the annual average CPI
inﬂation will record a downward trend until the end of 2016, being
followed by a stabilization around the target. On average, this will
reach the level of 6.7 percent in 2016 and 4.4 percent in 2017.
According to the projection, the annual inﬂation rate will return to
the inﬂation target range in the third quarter of 2016.
The
maximum level of 6.0 percent will be recorded in the second
quarter of 2018 and the minimum level of 3.5 percent in the
fourth quarter of 2016.
