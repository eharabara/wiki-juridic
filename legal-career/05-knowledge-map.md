# 05. Knowledge map: the LLM Wiki

Status: active. Last revised 2026-09-04.

This document records what the legal knowledge layer actually contains, so that answers can be honest about their own foundations. It is the reference behind the confidence line required by document 03.

## What the wiki is

A structured knowledge base of Moldovan legislation, built to be queried by a language model rather than read by a person. It lives as an Obsidian vault at `C:\Users\harab\wiki`, governed by its own `SCHEMA.md` and `CLAUDE.md`.

Its original perimeter was the legislation supervised by the National Financial Market Commission (CNPF). That perimeter has been passed. The base now also carries the Civil Code, company law, the Fiscal Code and the Administrative Code.

The build has produced substantive findings of its own, not just an index. Three in particular:

- **Mandate boundaries.** Where CNPF acts extend beyond what the primary law confers. This is a legal vulnerability in the acts concerned, and a usable argument. Since the Administrative Code 116/2018 was ingested, the argument can be carried through to the remedy rather than stopping at the diagnosis.
- **Transposition gaps.** Where the Moldovan text departs from the EU act it was meant to carry.
- **Acquis alignment.** How the perimeter as a whole sits against the EU financial services framework.

## The layer structure

- **Raw layer.** The source text of each act, ingested from legis.md, anchored at article level.
- **Structured layer.** The organisation of that text into a queryable form: 70 pages listed in `index.md`, of which 38 entities and 24 concepts.
- **Findings layer.** The analytical conclusions drawn on top, including the three above.
- **Control layer.** Added 2026-09-04. The machinery in `_meta/` that lets the other three be checked rather than trusted: the lint, the anchoring verification chain, and the in-force register. This layer exists because the first three can all be wrong quietly, and a knowledge base whose errors are silent is worse than one whose gaps are visible.

## Where the base stands

**Anchored at article level.** The 15 CNPF and BNM perimeter laws. The Civil Code 1107/2002, 2,657 article anchors plus 381 structural ones. Law 100/2017 on normative acts. Company law: 135/2007, 220/2007, 845/1992, and 1134/1997 on joint stock companies. The Fiscal Code 1163/1997, 512 anchors. The Administrative Code 116/2018, 260 anchors. Government decisions 1170/2016 and 1171/2018, correctly carrying zero article anchors because they are structured in numbered points.

**Present but structured rather than full text.** 29 EU acts as extracts. This is by design, not a defect, but it means a transposition finding rests on a summary of the EU side, not on its full text.

**Outside coverage entirely.** Seven codes: procedură civilă 225/2003, executare 443/2004, vamal 95/2021, muncii 154/2003, contravențional 218/2008, penal 985/2002, procedură penală 122/2003. Case law of any kind, including Constitutional Court decisions that annul provisions. Answers in these areas rest on general legal knowledge and must be marked as such.

## The three ways an answer can still be wrong

The earlier version of this document described one failure mode, incompleteness. There are three, and they fail with decreasing loudness. This is the part of the map worth reading twice.

**One, the provision is not in the base.** The wiki returns something plausible and the governing provision never appears. This is what the confidence line in document 03 was built for, and it remains the most common failure. It is also the least dangerous of the three, because coverage is knowable and this document records it.

**Two, the provision is in the base but the text is old.** Largely closed as of 2026-09-04. Of 23 acts ingested from legis.md, 21 carry a consolidation less than two years old. Two do not: `L-178-2020` at 2020-09-18, and `HG-1171-2018` at 2024-07-05. Age alone is not proof of staleness, since an unamended act legitimately keeps an old consolidation date. The only real test is whether legis.md now serves a different doc_id, and that test has never been run systematically.

**Three, the provision is in the base, current, anchored and hash-verified, and still is not the law today.** legis.md consolidations incorporate amendments whose entry into force is deferred, so the file describes law that has not arrived. Nothing in the anchor, the lint or the hash reveals this. The file looks more trustworthy than the two above, and is wrong in a way a reader cannot detect.

Five provisions are currently in this state, across three acts. The sharpest is Law 171/2012 on the capital market, articles 38 and 141^1, shown as repealed and in force until 1 June 2027. The register at `_meta/inforce/in-force-register.md` lists all five, and document 03 section 4 makes consulting it mandatory before citing an article.

## The file that was unmeasurable, and now is not

The Civil Code is the largest and most load-bearing text in the base. Until 4 September 2026 it had no version identity: no `source_url`, no `doc_id`, no `consolidation_date`. It came from a supplied consolidated PDF rather than from legis.md, which left it invisible to both controls. The staleness check works by comparing doc_id values, and the in-force check by scanning legis.md consolidation markers. The Civil Code carried neither, so it appeared in neither audit, not because it was clean but because it could not be measured.

It was measured on that date, and the result inverted the expectation. The legis.md record is doc_id 150561, consolidated as at 1 November 2025. The wiki text applies LP251/2025, in force from 1 April 2026, which the legis.md consolidation does not carry. The two article inventories differ by exactly the six articles LP251 repealed, and no article is present in the wiki and absent from legis.md.

So the wiki holds the more current text, and the risk runs the other way. A refresh from doc_id 150561 would roll the Civil Code back five months and reinstate six repealed articles. The frontmatter now blocks that operation explicitly.

The residual unknown is narrower and worth stating rather than dropping. The check compared the inventory of articles, not the wording of each one, and LP251 also reworded provisions. And a single legis.md record was confirmed by act-number search, not by exhaustive enumeration of the versions legis.md may hold.

The general lesson is worth more than the particular result. A file that cannot be measured is not a file that is probably wrong. It is a file about which nothing can be said, and the honest response is to make it measurable rather than to assume in either direction.

## Known gaps and unproven findings

These are tracked in the vault's `CLAUDE.md`, under "Open findings that need Eugen's judgement", and are not duplicated here so that the two do not drift apart. In summary they are: numbering gaps with no recorded repeal in `L-192-1998`, `L-171-2012`, `L-100-2017`, `L-220-2007`, `L-845-1992` and the Fiscal Code; repeals marked at chapter level with no repealing act named, in the Civil Code and the Fiscal Code; superscript flattening at paragraph and letter level, which corrupts a citation rather than losing it; and 89 English-language BNM files with 3,597 article markers and no anchors, so any answer resting on the English BNM corpus is not anchored.

## Planned extension

The two directions recorded in the first version of this document, the Civil Code and policy development, are both done, and the Civil Code's version identity was settled on 4 September 2026. What replaces them, in order of consequence rather than convenience:

1. **Run the currency check as a sweep.** Re-query legis.md for the current doc_id of every ingested act and compare. This is mechanical and has never been run across the corpus. The Civil Code result shows why it matters in both directions: the sweep must record where legis.md is behind, not only where the wiki is.
2. **Constitutional Court decisions.** Small volume, high error cost. A provision annulled by the Curtea Constituțională still sits in the raw text reading as law.
3. **EU acts in full text** for the perimeter, so a transposition finding cites both sides rather than one.
4. **The remaining seven codes**, when a matter calls for them.

## Working priority

The old rule here was to finish anchoring the perimeter before extending. That rule has been satisfied and is retired.

Its replacement is different in kind. Extension is no longer the constraint, verification is. Every act added to the base adds a provision that can be stale, deferred, or annulled, and the three checks above are what keep the confidence line meaning something. So the rule is: no ingestion without the control that covers it. When an act enters, the register is regenerated and the lint is run in the same pass, not later.

## Update rule

When a batch of acts is ingested or anchored, record here what changed. When a gap is discovered in use, record it here immediately, even before it is fixed. A known gap is manageable. An unknown one is not.
