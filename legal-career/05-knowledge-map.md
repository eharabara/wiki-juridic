# 05. Knowledge map: the LLM Wiki

Status: active. Last revised 2026-09-05.

This document records what the legal knowledge layer actually contains, so that answers can be honest about their own foundations. It is the reference behind the confidence line required by document 03.

## Why this document was wrong, and what changed because of it

The version of 4 September said seven codes were "outside coverage entirely": procedură civilă, executare, vamal, muncii, contravențional, penal, procedură penală. All seven were already ingested and anchored the following day. The document was wrong within a day of being written, and wrong in the direction that wastes the base: it understated coverage, so an answer would have been marked not anchored when the text was present, anchored and verifiable.

The cause is the one the vault already solved for itself. The vault's `CLAUDE.md` carries a coverage table generated from the files by `_meta/coverage/build_coverage.py`, precisely because a hand-written description drifts within hours when more than one agent writes to the folder. This document restated those counts by hand and inherited the drift.

So the rule changes here. **This document no longer restates what the generated table holds.** It names where the table lives and records only what a script cannot produce: judgement, failure modes, and priority. For the current inventory, read the coverage table in the vault's `CLAUDE.md`, or run the script.

## What the wiki is

A structured knowledge base of Moldovan legislation, built to be queried by a language model rather than read by a person. It lives as an Obsidian vault at `C:\Users\harab\wiki`, under local git since 5 September, governed by its own `SCHEMA.md` and `CLAUDE.md`.

Its original perimeter was the legislation supervised by the National Financial Market Commission (CNPF). That perimeter has been passed twice over. The base now carries the Civil Code, company law, the Fiscal Code, the Administrative Code, all seven remaining codes, the banking perimeter in Romanian, and a declared policy perimeter.

Since 5 September the vault holds two declared perimeters, `legal` and `policy`, distinguished by a frontmatter field and by different citation rules in `SCHEMA.md`.

## The layer structure

- **Raw layer.** The source text of each act, ingested from legis.md, anchored at article level.
- **Structured layer.** The pages listed in `index.md`, split between the two perimeters, under `entities/`, `concepts/`, `comparisons/` and `queries/`. `queries/` is empty since the EMIR drafting pages were archived. `index.md` holds the count. It is not repeated here.
- **Findings layer.** The analytical conclusions drawn on top: mandate boundaries, transposition gaps, acquis alignment.
- **Control layer.** The machinery in `_meta/` that lets the other three be checked rather than trusted: the validator, the anchoring verification chain, the coverage generator and the in-force register.

## The findings layer is now the weakest layer

This inverts the working assumption of the previous version, which treated the raw layer as the fragile part.

All 17 `acquis-*` concept pages and the transposition matrix carry `confidence: medium`. Sixteen of those eighteen were last updated on 9 July 2026, before the raw layer was rebuilt in September. The ground moved under them and they were never re-checked. The matrix itself still carries `imported_from` a July ancestor folder.

The asymmetry underneath is measurable. These are measurements rather than inventory, taken on 5 September. The Moldovan perimeter laws hold 14,161 lines of full text. The 29 EU acts hold 4,960 lines in total, an average of 171 lines each, because they are structured extracts. MiFID II sits in the base as roughly 170 lines against 2,611 lines of Law 171/2012.

Two consequences follow, and both are causal rather than cosmetic. A transposition finding compares a full Moldovan text against a summary of the EU text, so a gap classified as "not transposed" may be a gap in the extract. And the page recording the comparison predates the current version of the Moldovan text, so even the Moldovan side of the comparison is not the text the base now holds.

Neither fact is visible in the finding itself. It reads as finished work. That is the same failure shape as the three below.

## The three ways an answer can still be wrong

They fail with decreasing loudness. This is the part of the map worth reading twice.

**One, the provision is not in the base.** Still the most common, and this document is what makes it knowable. The correction at the top shows the error runs in both directions: an area recorded as uncovered when it is covered is as costly as the reverse, because it downgrades an answer that was in fact anchored.

**Two, the provision is in the base but the text is old.** The generated table flags the acts whose consolidation is more than two years old. One limit is worth stating rather than discovering later. The staleness check skips any act whose frontmatter `latest_modification_line` begins with "Publicat", which the script reads as never amended. The rule is sound, since an unamended act legitimately keeps an old consolidation date, and it is why `L-178-2020`, consolidated 2020-09-18, does not appear as stale. But it rests on a scraped display line, and it removes an act from the check silently. Worth one verification pass. The systematic test, re-querying legis.md for the current doc_id of every ingested act, still has never been run.

**Three, the provision is in the base, current, anchored and hash-verified, and still is not the law today.** The register at `_meta/inforce/in-force-register.md` carries the current count in its own header line. The scale is what matters here: on 4 September this document recorded five provisions across three acts, and within a day the true figure was an order of magnitude larger. Document 03 section 4 makes consulting the register mandatory before citing.

Two limits of the register, both recorded in the vault's `CLAUDE.md` and both live:

- It does not read acts structured in points. `HG-743-2024` has six deferred provisions and the register collapses them into one entry labelled with an article number that does not exist in the act. Government decisions are cited by point, so this affects a whole class of act. Until it is fixed, read the amendment brackets in the raw file for any hotărâre de Guvern.
- Where a deferred amendment rewrites an article instead of repealing it, the version in force today is absent from the file altogether. No marker can supply a text that is not there, and the answer is not anchored whatever the anchor says.

## The Civil Code, and the general lesson

Until 4 September the Civil Code had no version identity, so it appeared in neither the staleness nor the in-force audit, not because it was clean but because it could not be measured. Measured on that date, the result inverted the expectation: the wiki text applies LP251/2025 in force from 1 April 2026, which the legis.md consolidation doc_id 150561 does not carry. A refresh from that record would roll the code back five months and reinstate six repealed articles. The frontmatter now blocks that operation.

The residual unknown is narrower and stays stated: the check compared the inventory of articles, not the wording of each, and LP251 also reworded provisions.

The lesson is worth more than the result. A file that cannot be measured is not a file that is probably wrong. It is a file about which nothing can be said, and the response is to make it measurable rather than to assume in either direction.

## Known gaps and unproven findings

Tracked in the vault's `CLAUDE.md` under "Open questions that need Eugen's judgement", and not duplicated here so the two do not drift apart.

One item from that list belongs here, because it changes how every other gap is read. **Refreshing an act to a newer legis.md consolidation destroys the amendment markers of earlier amendments.** Found on 5 September while verifying `L-234-2016`, which looked defective and was not: art. 24 and the whole of Chapter IV were repealed by LP292/2023, and the markers proving it vanished when the act was refreshed to its 2024 consolidation. So a numbering gap with no marker means "no marker in this consolidation", never "no repeal". The wiki's own refresh turned a properly documented act into an apparently defective one, and the same is presumed of the remaining unexplained gaps until each is compared against an earlier version.

## Working priority

The rule of 4 September said extension is no longer the constraint, verification is. That holds, and 5 September sharpened what it means.

The verification that is missing is not of the raw layer. That layer passes every generated check: validator at zero errors, every raw source verified by sha256, coverage and in-force register current. It is the layer built on top that is unverified, and no script can check it, because judgement is not mechanical.

So the rule has two halves now. No ingestion without the control that covers it. And no new finding while the existing findings are unverified.

## Update rule

When a batch of acts is ingested or anchored, record here what changed. When a gap is discovered in use, record it here immediately, even before it is fixed. A known gap is manageable, an unknown one is not.

And, added 5 September: do not restate a figure the coverage script generates. Name the table and record the judgement.
