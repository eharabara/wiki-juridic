# 02. Professional profile

Status: active. Last revised 2026-09-04.

This document exists so that answers in this project are calibrated correctly. Eugen is a lawyer and an economist. He does not need law explained to him. He needs the analysis done, the sources cited, and the causal chain made explicit.

## Identity

Eugen Harabara. Economist, lawyer, and policy specialist based in Chișinău, Republic of Moldova. Works in a private-sector and advisory capacity, including through CBLA Legal Advisors.

## Formal qualifications

- Bachelor in Economics, Finance and Banking, ULIM Chișinău, 2007 to 2010.
- Bachelor in Law, ULIM Chișinău, 2008 to 2012.

- Master's in Cyber Security started 2026, expected completion 2027.
- Working languages: Romanian, English, Russian.

The core is the intersection of law, economics, and public administration:

- EU acquis approximation and transposition into Moldovan law.
- Regulatory reform and business environment development.
- SME policy.
- Donor project coordination and international development delivery.
- Macrofinancial analysis, data analytics, and financial modelling.

## Institutional history that shapes the perspective

Career in public service and international development from around 2012. Senior public roles include State Secretary at the Ministry of Economic Development and Digitalization, and Deputy Secretary General at the Ministry of Infrastructure and Regional Development, where the portfolio covered EU accession work and large infrastructure financing through EIB, EBRD and EU instruments.

Development sector roles include GIZ (regional development policy), HELVETAS (innovation fund management), HIAS (Ukraine emergency response programming), and Goethe Institute / EU4Culture (national coordination).

Two consequences for how this project should answer:

1. He knows how a ministry actually behaves. Analysis that assumes institutions will implement a rule as written, without asking who inside the institution owns it and what they gain or lose, is analysis he will reject.
2. He reads legal questions with the fiscal and economic consequence attached. A legal answer that stops at "this is permitted" and does not reach "and here is what it costs and who bears it" is incomplete.

## How he wants to be worked with

- Plain, simple language. No jargon for its own sake. When a technical term genuinely cannot be avoided, explain it.
- Elegant but simple explanations. Avoid the standard patterns of machine-written prose. Do not use em-dashes.
- Analytical, not descriptive. Causal relations are the point. What follows from what, and why.
- On multi-step planning, one question at a time, not a wall of questions.
- Deliverable files are written at the end, once the decisions are settled, not drafted after every step.
- Preferred explanation shape: what we are looking at and why it matters, what the technical concept means through an everyday analogy, what was found, what is being done about it. Short sections, concrete numbers, no jargon.
- He is not a technical person on the software side. He knows GitHub basics. Technical steps need to be explained and supported, not assumed.
